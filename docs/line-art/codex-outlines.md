# Codex brief: Mammals outline pictures for Colouring and Draw & Trace (25 November 2026)

Mammal Discovery Club (the Mammals app, ages 4–7) has two Arts & Crafts activities that need clean outline drawings. Today both are empty.

- **Colouring:** children tap a space to fill it with colour, so every shape must be fully closed.
- **Draw & Trace:** the app finds the middle of every line and replays it as if a hand were drawing it. Children watch, then trace it step by step with a finger. The same pictures become printed trace-and-colour sheets.

The **colour of each line sets the drawing order**, so use exactly the palette below and nothing else. You make the artwork only; Claude converts it and wires it in.

**Work one picture at a time.** If a picture comes out wrong, redraw the whole picture.

## Shared style (every picture)

- Children's "how to draw" line art: friendly, rounded and simple, like a colouring-book page. Friendly faces, but true to the real animal's shape.
- Outlines only: **one clean stroke per edge**, even line weight, round ends and round joins.
  - Easy and Medium: about 10 px wide. Hard: about 8 px wide.
- No fills, shading, hatching, fur texture, gradients, glow, sketchy or wobbly double lines, or overlapping strokes. Show fur, spines, stripes and wool with a few clean lines or a scalloped edge, never with hatching.
- Lines that touch must really join (a leg meets the body, an ear meets the head, a tail meets the body). Closed shapes are fully closed, with no gaps.
- Parts that should not touch keep a clear white gap: at least 14 px (Hard: at least 10 px).
- Pure flat white background (#FFFFFF), opaque, with no paper texture, border, frame or ground shadow.
- Subject centred and as large as the picture allows, with about 24–40 px of white margin on every side.
- No text, letters, numbers, labels or arrows.
- Size: **1024 × 768 PNG, landscape.**

## The science must be right

Children learn from these pictures, so count before you finish each one:

- **Four-legged mammals:** four legs, each ending in a clear paw, foot or hoof; two ears (both visible unless the view hides one); one eye in side view, two from the front; a tail where the animal really has one, of the right kind (a rabbit's short round scut, a pig's curly tail, a fox's bushy tail with a tip, a cow's tail with a tuft).
- **Hooved animals** (sheep, cow, deer, giraffe, zebra, pig): split or single hooves as in life, shown as a small black hoof line at the foot.
- **Whales and dolphins:** no legs and no gills; a horizontal tail (two flukes, side by side, not up and down like a fish); flippers; a blowhole on the top of the head. A dolphin has one curved fin on its back.
- **Seals** (grey or harbour seal): front flippers with little claws, back flippers pointing backwards, **no ear flaps** (just a small ear hole), whiskers.
- **Bats** (pipistrelle): each wing is skin stretched between long finger bones, with a small hooked thumb at the top front edge; big ears; two small back feet.
- **Deer:** only the male (stag) has antlers; antlers are branched bone, not horns.
- **Lion:** the male has a mane; a lioness has none.
- **Elephant:** African elephant with big ears, a trunk with a tip, two tusks.
- **Giraffe:** two short horn-like ossicones with round tips, a short upright mane along the neck, a tufted tail.
- **Hedgehog:** spines on the back and top of the head only; the face, legs and belly are fur (no spines).
- **Red squirrel:** ear tufts, a big bushy tail.
- **Kangaroo:** a joey's head and front paws peeping out of the mother's pouch on her belly; big back feet, small front arms, a long thick tail.
- **Otter:** a long body, small round ears, whiskers, webbed feet, a long thick tail that tapers.
- **Beaver:** a flat, wide, paddle-shaped tail; two big front teeth.
- **Badger:** a stripe down each side of the face, small ears with pale tips, short strong legs with claws.
- British wildlife where the subject is British. No clothes, no people, no human hands.

## Difficulty levels

- **Easy:** 5 to 12 lines. Big simple shapes a 4-year-old can trace. One animal on its own (at most a ground line).
- **Medium:** 12 to 22 lines. One animal with more parts, or an animal with a little scene, or two simple animals.
- **Hard:** 22 to 40 lines. A small scene or several animals. Still clean and uncrowded; no tiny fiddly bits under 20 px.
